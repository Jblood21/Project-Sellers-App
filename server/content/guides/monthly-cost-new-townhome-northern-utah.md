Principal and interest is the number people quote. It's usually about two thirds of what actually leaves your account.

## What makes up the monthly payment?

A townhome payment in Davis or Weber County is built out of principal and interest, property taxes, homeowner's insurance, mortgage insurance if your program requires it, and the HOA. Miss any one of them and your estimate is wrong by enough to matter.

On a $399,900 purchase with 5 percent down, principal and interest is the largest line by a wide margin. But taxes, insurance and an HOA in the $170 range together can add several hundred dollars a month, and mortgage insurance can add more on top of that.

## How do property taxes work on new construction?

This one catches people. A newly built home is often assessed on land value alone in its first tax year, then reassessed once the structure is complete. Your escrow payment can rise noticeably at the first adjustment even though nothing about your loan changed.

Ask what the current assessed value is and what the full assessed value is expected to be. Budget against the second number, not the first.

## Is the HOA part of my mortgage payment?

As an example, dues at one new Davis County townhome community are $172 per month. The HOA is billed by the association, not collected in your mortgage escrow, so it won't appear on your loan statement at all. Add it to the payment yourself when you compare against a detached house with no HOA.

> **$172 a month is $2,064 a year** — Roughly what a blanket policy, common-area maintenance and shared amenities cost when they're split across a community.

## How does insurance work in a townhome community?

Because the association carries a blanket property policy, your individual policy covers less than it would on a detached house, and it usually costs less as a result. Get an actual quote rather than using a rule of thumb, because the split between the blanket policy and your HO-6 varies by association.

## When do I pay mortgage insurance?

With less than 20 percent down on a conventional loan there's monthly mortgage insurance, priced off your credit score and your loan-to-value. FHA has its own structure with an upfront premium and an annual one. VA has no monthly mortgage insurance at all. USDA has a guarantee fee.

This is the line most online calculators either omit or guess at. Get a real figure for your credit profile before you decide what you can afford.

## How do I build my own estimate?

Take the actual price of the actual home. Enter the rate you're actually being quoted, not a headline rate. Add the $172. Add a tax estimate based on the completed value. Add a real insurance quote and a real mortgage insurance figure. What you get is a number you can plan against.
