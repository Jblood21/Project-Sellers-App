There's a minimum to qualify, and then there's the score that decides what you pay. Two very different conversations.

## What credit score do I need to qualify?

Each loan program sets its own floor, and individual lenders can set stricter requirements on top of it. FHA is generally the most accommodating on score, conventional sits higher, and VA has no published minimum although lenders apply their own.

Program minimums change, so treat any number you read online as a starting point and get your actual file evaluated rather than assuming you don't qualify.

## How does my score change what I pay?

Conventional loans price by credit score and loan-to-value together. Move up a scoring tier and both your rate and your monthly mortgage insurance can improve. On a loan near $380,000, the difference between tiers isn't trivial, and it lasts as long as you keep the loan.

> **Twenty points can be worth thousands** — Ask your loan officer what the next scoring tier would change on your specific file before you assume it isn't worth chasing.

## What raises a credit score in ninety days?

**Utilization.** Card balances relative to limits carry heavy weight and update monthly. Paying a card from 80 percent utilization down to under 30 can move a score meaningfully within one or two reporting cycles.

**Errors.** Collections that aren't yours, accounts reporting as open that were closed, wrong balances. Dispute them.

**Not opening anything new.** A new card or an auto loan during your home search does two things at once: it adds an inquiry and it adds a payment to your debt-to-income ratio.

## What won’t fix my score quickly?

Closing old accounts, which shortens your credit history. Paying off a collection, which sometimes helps and sometimes does nothing depending on the scoring model. And waiting, unless there's something specific aging off.

## When should I check my credit before shopping?

A lender pulls a mortgage-specific score, which is often different from the one your banking app shows. Have it pulled early. If there's a ninety-day fix worth making, ninety days before you shop is when you want to know.
