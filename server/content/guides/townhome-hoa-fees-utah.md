The dues figure tells you very little on its own. What it covers, and whether the association is funded for what it owns, tells you everything.

## What do HOA dues pay for?

In an attached-home community, the association typically maintains the exterior of the buildings, the common landscaping, the interior streets and the shared amenities, and it usually carries a blanket property insurance policy on the structures. Some communities include snow removal and some don't. Some include exterior paint on a schedule and some assess for it separately.

None of that's standard. It's written in the CC&Rs, and the CC&Rs are the only thing that governs.

## How does the HOA’s blanket policy change my insurance?

Because the association insures the structure, your own policy covers the interior and your belongings rather than the whole building. That's usually cheaper than insuring a detached house, but you still need a policy, and the exact dividing line varies. Ask your agent to quote an HO-6 for the specific community.

## Why do HOA reserves matter?

An association that maintains roofs, siding, paving and amenities has to save for the day those things need replacing. That's what reserves are. An underfunded reserve doesn't show up in the monthly dues, it shows up years later as a special assessment or a sudden increase.

> **Ask for the reserve study** — If one exists, it tells you what the association owns, what it will cost to replace, and how much has been set aside.

## Which HOA documents should I request?

- **The CC&Rs and bylaws.** What's covered, what you can and can't do to your own home, and what the rental rules are.
- **The current budget.** Where the dues go, line by line.
- **The reserve study.** Whether the association is funded for what it owns.
- **The last few sets of meeting minutes.** Whether a dues increase or a special assessment has already been discussed.

## Do rental restrictions matter if I’m living there?

Many associations cap the percentage of homes that can be rented. That protects owner-occupant character, and it also affects financing: some loan programs won't lend in a community above a certain rental ratio. If you might rent the home out later, or if you want to know your buyer pool isn't restricted when you sell, read that section carefully.

## What does this look like at a new community?

As an example, one new Davis County townhome community sets dues at $172 per month, and the association carries a blanket property policy on the buildings. Everything above still applies. Request the current documents from the seller or listing brokerage and read them before you remove contingencies.
