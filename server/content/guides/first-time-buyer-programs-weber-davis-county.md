Assistance is out there. Whether you can use it comes down to your income, the purchase price, the property, and whether the program still has money this quarter.

## Is down-payment assistance a grant or a loan?

Most down-payment assistance in Utah is structured as a second mortgage. Some are deferred and forgiven after you occupy the home for a set number of years. Some carry a payment from day one. A few are true grants, and those are the ones that run out fastest.

Read the structure before you fall in love with the headline. A second mortgage with a monthly payment affects your debt-to-income ratio and therefore how much first mortgage you qualify for.

## Who counts as a first-time buyer?

Typically it means you haven't owned a primary residence in the past three years, not that you've never owned a home. Rules vary by program and some have no first-time requirement at all.

## What limits decide whether I qualify?

- **Household income.** Usually capped as a percentage of area median income for the county, and it counts everyone in the household, not just the borrowers.
- **Purchase price.** Many programs cap the price of the home.
- **Property location and type.** Some programs are county-specific, and some exclude certain property types.

> **Funding is the fourth limit** — Programs run on allocations. Eligibility on paper means nothing if the current round is exhausted.

## How does new construction change the timing?

If the home is still being built, your closing date may be months out. Assistance reservations and rate locks both have expiry dates. Ask specifically how the program handles a delayed completion before you rely on it.

## Can I combine assistance with a seller credit?

You can sometimes combine assistance with a seller-paid credit, and sometimes you can't, because programs limit total contributions and loan programs cap seller contributions separately. Both need to be checked against the same transaction rather than evaluated on their own.

## How do I check my eligibility quickly?

Give a lender your household income, your target price and the county. That's enough to tell you in one conversation which programs are worth an application and which aren't. Program details change often enough that anything published, including this page, needs verifying against current guidelines.
