# What closing costs actually are

*Closing costs*

*Educational information, not financial or legal advice*

Closing costs aren't one fee. They're about a dozen, paid to five or six different parties, and only some of them are negotiable.

## What do closing costs actually include?

**Lender fees.** Origination, underwriting, and any discount points you choose to buy. These are the ones that vary between lenders and the ones worth comparing.

**Third-party fees.** Appraisal, credit report, title search, title insurance, settlement or escrow fees, and recording fees. Mostly fixed by the vendor or the county.

**Prepaids and escrow.** Not fees at all. This is your own money moving forward: the first year of homeowner's insurance, prepaid interest from closing to the end of the month, and the initial deposit into your tax and insurance escrow account.

## What are prepaids?

Prepaids can be the largest single line on the closing statement, and they feel like a cost. They aren't. You would pay that insurance premium and those property taxes whether you financed the home or not. Closing simply front-loads some of it.

It matters because prepaids depend on your closing date. Close at the end of the month and you owe less prepaid interest. Close on the first and you owe nearly a full month.

## Which closing costs are negotiable?

Lender fees, discount points, and who pays for what between buyer and seller. Title and escrow charges are sometimes negotiable depending on the provider. Appraisal, credit and recording fees aren't.

> **Compare loan estimates side by side** — Every lender is required to give you one in the same format. That's the document to compare, not the rate quoted over the phone.

## How does a seller credit apply to closing costs?

A seller-paid credit can cover eligible closing costs and prepaids. It can't exceed the actual costs, so a large credit against a small closing statement leaves money on the table unless it's restructured toward the rate or the price.

Loan programs also cap how much a seller may contribute, and for conventional financing the cap depends on your down payment. That's why a $20,000 advertised incentive doesn't automatically arrive as $20,000 in your column, and why it needs to be priced against a specific home and a specific program.

## When should I ask for a closing cost estimate?

You're entitled to a loan estimate within three business days of a complete application. Get one before you're emotionally committed to a house. It's much easier to compare lenders in week one than in week five.
