# 16 questions to ask before you sign

*Checklist*

By Dillan Lewis, NMLS #1337516 · Summit Home Loans LLC, NMLS #1790749

*Educational information, not financial or legal advice*

![New construction townhome living, dining and kitchen](images/blog-hero-model-home.webp)

Print it and take it with you. Get straight answers to all sixteen and you know exactly what you're buying.

## What should I ask about the home itself?

1. What's the exact square footage of this unit, and does it include the garage?
1. Which bath configuration does this specific unit have: walk-in shower, garden tub, or a standard tub and shower insert?
1. What's on the included-feature list for this home, in writing, and what was an upgrade?
1. What's the estimated completion date, and what happens in the contract if it slips?
1. Are appliances included, and which ones?

## What should I ask about the HOA?

1. What are the current dues, and when were they last increased?
1. What exactly do the dues cover, and what does the blanket policy insure?
1. Is there a reserve study, and is the association funded against it?
1. Are there rental restrictions or caps?
1. Has a special assessment been discussed in any recent meeting?

> **Ask for documents, not answers** — The CC&Rs, the budget, the reserve study and the minutes. Verbal assurances aren't enforceable.

## What should I ask about the community?

1. What's planned for the remaining phases, and on what timeline?
1. Which amenities are built, which are funded, and which are still concepts?
1. Who maintains the interior streets, and are they public or private?

## What should I ask about the money?

1. Is the incentive tied to a specific lender, and what is the total cost if I use a different one?
1. How long can I lock a rate, what does an extension cost, and is there a float-down?
1. What's the warranty, what does it exclude, and when does each tier expire?

## How do I use these questions?

Take it to the model. Write the answers down next to the questions, with the date and the name of who told you. Then compare what you were told against the documents you receive. Where they differ, the documents govern, and the gap is worth a conversation before you remove contingencies.
