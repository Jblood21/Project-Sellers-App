# How much house can you afford?

*Financing*

By your listing agent

*Educational information, not financial or legal advice*

Two different numbers matter: what a lender will approve, and what you are comfortable paying. They are often not the same.

## Should I think about price or payment?

Payment. A home's price is just the starting point. What you live with every month is the full payment, including taxes and insurance, plus the cost of keeping the home running. Two homes at the same price can have very different monthly costs.

Try See My Payment for this home to see an estimate, or See What I Can Afford to work from your income and debts.

## What is debt-to-income?

Debt-to-income, or DTI, compares what you owe each month to what you earn each month before taxes. Add up your monthly debt payments (car, student loans, credit cards, and the new housing payment), then divide by your gross monthly income.

Lenders use DTI as one signal of how much you can handle. The limits depend on the loan type and your whole file, so ask a lender about theirs. As a rule of thumb, the lower your DTI, the more room you have.

## What do I need in cash?

Plan for more than the down payment:

- **Down payment.** Varies by loan type.
- **Closing costs.** As a rule of thumb, commonly a few percent of the price.
- **Moving and setup.** Movers, cleaning, locks, basic furniture.
- **Repairs and first-year surprises.** Even a good home has some.

## What is the cushion?

The cushion is the money left over after you buy. Lenders approve based on numbers, but they don't know your life. A cushion covers a job gap, a medical bill or a failed water heater.

A useful rule of thumb is to keep several months of living expenses in savings after closing, plus a maintenance reserve. Keeping about 1% of the home's value per year for upkeep is a common guideline for a detached home. These are rules of thumb, not requirements.

## What should I check besides the mortgage?

- Property taxes, which can differ a lot from one home to the next
- Homeowner's insurance
- Utilities for a larger home
- HOA dues, if any
- Yard and exterior care you now handle yourself

## What is a good way to decide?

1. Write down what you pay now for housing and what you save.
2. Decide what monthly payment still lets you save and enjoy life.
3. Use that payment to find a price range, not the other way round.
4. Ask a lender what they would approve, and compare.

An estimate is not a quote or approval. A lender can give you a real loan estimate once you apply.
