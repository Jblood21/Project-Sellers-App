# Down payment options for a first home

*Financing*

By your listing agent

*Educational information, not financial or legal advice*

You may not need as much for a down payment as you think, and you may not need all of it from your own savings. The right choice depends on your situation, so this guide stays general.

## What are the main loan types?

**Conventional loans.** Not backed by a government agency. Some allow a small down payment, and the amount you put down can affect your cost and whether you pay mortgage insurance.

**FHA loans.** Backed by the Federal Housing Administration. They are often considered by buyers with smaller savings or lower credit scores. They come with mortgage insurance.

**VA loans.** For eligible veterans, service members and some surviving spouses. They can allow buying with no down payment. Eligibility depends on service history.

**USDA loans.** For eligible homes in eligible areas and for buyers within income limits. Not every property qualifies, so ask whether this home does.

Each program has its own rules about credit, income, property condition and limits. Those details change, so ask a lender for current ones.

## How does a bigger down payment help?

Generally, putting more down means a smaller loan, a lower monthly payment and sometimes lower costs. But it also uses cash you may need for repairs and a cushion. A bigger down payment is not automatically better if it leaves you with nothing in the bank.

## Can someone give me money for it?

Often, yes, in the form of gift funds, usually from a close relative. Lenders typically want a signed gift letter and a paper trail showing where the money came from. Rules vary by loan type. Tell your lender early rather than moving money around first.

## What about help programs?

Down payment and closing cost assistance exists in many places. Some come from state or local housing agencies, some from employers, and some from nonprofits. They may have income limits, training requirements or rules about the home's location. Funding runs out and eligibility changes, so I can't list them here. Ask a lender and your agent what is open where you live.

## How do I compare options?

1. Ask two or three lenders for a loan estimate for the same home price.
2. Compare the monthly payment and the cash needed to close, not just the rate.
3. Check whether mortgage insurance applies and for how long.
4. Ask about assistance you might combine with the loan.
