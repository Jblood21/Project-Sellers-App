# Get pre-approved before you make an offer

*Financing*

By your listing agent

*Educational information, not financial or legal advice*

Pre-approval is the step that turns "I'd like to buy" into "I'm ready to buy." Do it before you make an offer, ideally before you tour many homes.

## What is the difference between pre-qualification and pre-approval?

**Pre-qualification** is a quick estimate based on what you tell a lender. It can take minutes and usually involves no document checks. It is useful for rough planning.

**Pre-approval** is deeper. The lender reviews your documents and credit, and issues a letter saying how much they are likely willing to lend, subject to conditions. It is not a final loan approval, because the home still has to be appraised and your file verified.

## What documents will I need?

Lenders vary, but expect to provide:

- Recent pay stubs and your last year or two of tax returns or W-2s
- Recent bank and savings statements
- Photo ID
- Details about debts, such as car loans and student loans
- Permission to check your credit
- If self-employed, more business paperwork

Gathering these in advance makes the process faster.

## How long does a pre-approval last?

Letters usually expire after a set period, commonly somewhere between 60 and 90 days. If yours runs out, the lender typically refreshes it. Ask yours how long it is valid.

## Why do sellers ask for it?

A pre-approval letter tells the seller your offer is backed by a real lender's review, not just good intentions. In a busy market, an offer without one may not be taken seriously. Many agents will ask to see a letter before writing an offer.

## Does it hurt my credit?

A lender will check your credit, which can cause a small, temporary dip. Several mortgage checks made within a short window are typically treated as one. Ask a lender how this works.

## Do I have to use a particular lender?

No. You can choose any lender you like, and it is smart to compare more than one. Look at the official loan estimate each one gives you, not just a quoted rate. Your agent may share names of lenders others have used, but the choice is yours.

## What should I do while pre-approved?

- Don't open new credit or make large purchases.
- Keep your job and income documents current.
- Don't move large sums of money around without being able to explain them.
- Remember that approval for a high amount does not mean you should borrow it. Use See What I Can Afford to find a payment that feels right to you.

To find out what you qualify for, talk to a lender. This guide cannot predict approval, rates or amounts.
