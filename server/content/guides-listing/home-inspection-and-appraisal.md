# Home inspection and appraisal

*Making an offer*

By your listing agent

*Educational information, not financial or legal advice*

These two checks are often mixed up. They do different jobs for different people.

## What is a home inspection?

An inspector, usually hired by you, examines the home's condition: roof, structure, plumbing, electrical, heating and cooling, and more. You get a written report with photos. It is for your benefit, so you know what you're buying.

The buyer typically orders and pays for it. Cost varies by home and area. Go along if you can, because the inspector will explain things in person.

Ask about extras too, depending on the home and region: sewer line, radon, pests, mold, a septic system or a well.

## What is an appraisal?

An appraiser, picked by your lender, estimates the home's market value. The lender wants to be sure the home is worth the loan. It is not an inspection, and it won't catch hidden defects. You usually pay the fee as part of your loan costs.

## What do I do with the inspection findings?

Not every finding is a deal-breaker. Sort them:

1. **Safety and major systems.** Roof, foundation, electrical, gas, water damage.
2. **Moderate repairs.** Worn systems nearing the end of their life.
3. **Cosmetic or minor.** Usually not worth negotiating.

Then, depending on your contract, you can ask the seller to repair, to credit you money, or to adjust the price, or you can accept the home as is. In some contracts you may also be able to cancel. Your agent will show you your deadlines and options. For serious structural questions, a specialist such as an engineer may help.

## What is the appraisal gap?

If the appraisal comes in lower than the agreed price, the lender will generally lend based on the lower value. The difference is the gap. Your options include:

- Asking the seller to lower the price
- Paying the difference in cash
- Splitting it with the seller
- Challenging the appraisal if there is a reason
- Walking away, if your contract has an appraisal contingency

Ask your agent whether to plan for the gap before you write your offer, especially in a competitive market.

## Should I skip the inspection?

Waiving it can make an offer look stronger, but it means you buy with unknown risks. Think carefully about what you can afford to fix.
