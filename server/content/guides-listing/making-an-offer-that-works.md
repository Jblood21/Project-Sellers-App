# Making an offer that works

*Making an offer*

By your listing agent

*Educational information, not financial or legal advice*

An offer is more than a price. It is a package of terms, and the strongest one is not always the highest.

## What goes into an offer?

**Price.** Your agent will show you recent sales of similar homes nearby so your number is based on evidence, not guesswork.

**Earnest money.** A good-faith deposit you put down once the offer is accepted. It is held by a neutral third party and usually goes toward your closing costs. Amounts vary by area. If you back out for a reason the contract doesn't allow, you can lose it.

**Contingencies.** Conditions that let you walk away or renegotiate. Common ones are inspection, financing and appraisal. Each one protects you, and each one makes your offer a bit less attractive to a seller. Your agent can explain the tradeoff.

**Closing date.** Sellers often care about timing. A date that fits their move can be worth as much as a few dollars.

**What's included.** Appliances, window coverings and similar items should be written down, not assumed.

**Seller concessions.** You can ask the seller to contribute toward your closing costs. Loan programs limit how much, and the seller may counter.

## What if there are multiple offers?

In a busy market a seller may get several at once. They may set a deadline for "best and final." Strengthening an offer can mean a larger deposit, flexible dates, or a shorter timeline. It should never mean skipping protections that you can't afford to lose, such as an inspection of some kind. Decide your limit before you start, and stay ready to walk away.

## What happens after I submit?

The seller can accept, reject or counter. A counteroffer changes some terms, and you can accept, counter back or decline. Once both sides sign, you are under contract, and the clock on your deadlines starts.

## How do I decide my number?

Before you write, check what the payment would be. Try See My Payment for this home at the price you have in mind. If that payment makes you nervous, the price is too high for you, whatever the market says.

## Why work with my agent here?

Contracts, deadlines and local customs matter, and mistakes are costly. Your agent writes the offer, explains each term and negotiates for you. Rules about contracts, deposits and disclosures differ by state. For legal questions about the contract, ask a real estate attorney.

If you are not yet working with an agent, say so, and ask how the relationship works before you sign anything.
