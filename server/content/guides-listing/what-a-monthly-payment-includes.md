# What a monthly payment includes

*Financing*

By your listing agent

*Educational information, not financial or legal advice*

The number people quote for a home is the price. The number you live with is the monthly payment, and it has more pieces than most people expect.

## What goes into a mortgage payment?

Lenders often describe it as PITI:

- **Principal.** The part that pays down what you borrowed.
- **Interest.** The cost of borrowing. Early on, most of each payment is interest.
- **Taxes.** Property tax set by your local government. It differs by county and can change.
- **Insurance.** Homeowner's insurance protects the house and your belongings. Lenders require it.

Many lenders collect taxes and insurance with your payment and pay the bills for you from an escrow account. Then you see them as part of one monthly amount.

## What else might be in there?

**Mortgage insurance.** Some loans with a smaller down payment require it. It protects the lender, not you. Ask how it works on the loan you are considering and when, if ever, it can go away.

**HOA dues.** If the home has a homeowners association, dues are a separate monthly or yearly cost. Lenders count them when figuring your payment.

## What does a detached home add that a payment doesn't show?

Your mortgage payment isn't your whole cost of living there. A detached home usually means:

- **Utilities.** Heating, cooling, water, sewer, trash. A larger home with a yard can cost more than an apartment.
- **Upkeep.** Roof, exterior, systems, appliances. As a rule of thumb, plan to keep about 1% of a home's value a year for maintenance.
- **Yard care.** Mowing, trees, irrigation, snow removal depending on where you live.

You are the landlord now, so there is no one else to call.

## Why do taxes and insurance change?

Property taxes can be reassessed, and insurance rates move with the market and your area. That means your payment can rise even with a fixed-rate loan, because only the principal and interest part is fixed. Ask your agent what the taxes look like for this home, and whether the current bill reflects the sale price.

## How do I see an estimate?

Try See My Payment for this home. It combines price, down payment and the other pieces into an estimate. It is an estimate, not a quote or a promise. A lender can give you an official loan estimate once you apply.

## What questions should I ask?

- What were the last year's property taxes, and could they change after a sale?
- What does insurance cost for a home like this?
- What are the typical utility bills?
- Is there an HOA, and what are the dues?

Rules differ by state. Your agent can tell you the local ones.
