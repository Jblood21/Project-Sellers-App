# What to expect at an open house

*Open houses*

By your listing agent

*Educational information, not financial or legal advice*

An open house is a set time when the home is open to anyone who wants to look. You don't need an appointment, and you don't need to be ready to buy. Looking is the whole point.

## How does an open house work?

You walk in, say hello, and usually sign in on a sheet or tablet. Take your time. The agent is there to open the door, answer questions and keep the home safe, not to push you toward an offer.

## Do I have to sign in?

Most hosts ask. It helps the seller know who visited, and it lets your agent follow up if you ask them to. Sign in only with details you are comfortable sharing, and ask what they will be used for. If you already work with your own agent, say so up front.

## What should I look at?

Walk the whole home once without judging, then go back and look closer:

- **Light and layout.** Does the floor plan fit how you live?
- **Water.** Stains on ceilings, soft spots under sinks, musty smells.
- **Cracks and doors.** Doors that stick or wide cracks in walls can point to settling or moisture.
- **Systems.** Ask the age of the roof, furnace, air conditioning and water heater.
- **Outside.** Drainage, fencing, the condition of the roof line and the yard.
- **Noise and parking.** Stand quietly in a few rooms and listen.

## What can I ask?

Anything. Good starters are why the owners are selling, how long it has been listed, and what is included in the sale. The guide "Questions to ask the listing agent" has a longer list.

## How do I keep notes?

Homes blur together after the third one. Take photos where allowed (ask first), jot down a few lines per home, and rate it right away. Note one thing you loved and one thing that worries you. Try See My Payment for this home to see roughly what it might cost each month, then write that number beside your notes.

## Is there any pressure?

There shouldn't be. You can leave without saying anything more than thanks. A good open house is a low-stakes way to learn what you like and what a home in your area is really like.

## What happens after?

If you like the home, tell the agent. You can request a second visit, ask for the seller's disclosures, or talk to a lender about what you can afford before making any offer. If you don't, nothing happens. Signing in is not a commitment to buy, to work with anyone, or to make an offer.

If you are serious, the next step is usually a pre-approval, covered in the guide "Get pre-approved before you make an offer."

Rules about open houses, disclosures and agency relationships differ by state. Your agent can tell you the local ones.
