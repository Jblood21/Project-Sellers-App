# Closing costs and cash to close

*Closing*

By your listing agent

*Educational information, not financial or legal advice*

Closing costs are the fees and prepayments due when your purchase is finalized. As a rule of thumb, they are commonly a few percent of the price, but your numbers will differ.

## What do closing costs include?

**Lender fees.** Charges for making the loan, such as origination and underwriting, and any points you choose to buy. These vary from lender to lender.

**Third-party fees.** Appraisal, credit report, title search, title insurance, escrow or settlement fees, recording fees, and sometimes an attorney. Many are set by outside companies or the local government.

**Prepaids and escrow.** These are not really fees. They are your own money paid in advance: the first year of homeowner's insurance, some property tax, interest from closing to month's end, and a starting deposit into an escrow account.

## What is cash to close?

It is the total you bring to closing: your down payment plus closing costs and prepaids, minus anything already paid (like earnest money) and any credits. Your lender's loan estimate shows it, and the final number is on your closing disclosure.

## What is negotiable?

- **Lender fees and points.** Compare lenders, and ask.
- **Who pays what.** You can ask the seller to contribute. Loan programs limit how much.
- **Title and escrow providers.** Sometimes you can shop around.
- **Often not negotiable:** government recording fees and taxes.

Never assume. Ask your lender and agent what is negotiable for your loan.

## How do I compare lenders?

Every lender must give you a loan estimate in the same format. Put two or three side by side and compare the total cash to close and the monthly payment, not just the rate.

## What happens on closing day?

1. You get your closing disclosure ahead of time. Read it and compare it to your loan estimate.
2. You do a final walk-through of the home, usually just before closing.
3. You sign the loan and sale documents, with a title or escrow officer, or an attorney, depending on where you live.
4. You send the funds, typically by wire or cashier's check. Always confirm wire instructions by phone using a number you already trust, since wire fraud is real.
5. The sale is recorded and you get the keys.

Bring photo ID. Don't open new credit or make big purchases beforehand.
