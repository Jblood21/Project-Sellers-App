# How buying a single-family home works

*Buying basics*

By your listing agent

*Educational information, not financial or legal advice*

Buying a house feels like one big event, but it is a short list of steps. Knowing the order takes much of the stress out of it.

## What are the steps from search to keys?

1. **Get your finances in shape.** Check your credit, save for a down payment and closing costs, and look at what monthly payment feels comfortable. See What I Can Afford is a good place to start.
2. **Get pre-approved.** A lender reviews your documents and tells you what they are likely to lend. See the guide on pre-approval.
3. **Search and visit homes.** Open houses and private showings help you learn what you want.
4. **Make an offer.** Your agent helps you write one with price, deposit, contingencies and dates.
5. **Negotiate.** The seller can accept, reject or counter. Counters are normal.
6. **Go under contract.** You put down earnest money, which is a good-faith deposit held by a neutral third party.
7. **Inspect and appraise.** You check the home's condition, and the lender checks its value.
8. **Finish the loan.** The lender verifies your file. Avoid new debt or big purchases during this stage.
9. **Close.** You sign final papers, pay your cash to close, and the sale is recorded.
10. **Get the keys.** Timing of possession is set in the contract, often at closing.

## How long does it take?

Searching can take weeks or months. From accepted offer to closing, a month or so is common with a loan, but it varies. Cash purchases and simple files can be faster.

## Who is on my team?

- **Your agent** guides the search, offer and negotiation.
- **A lender** of your choice provides the loan.
- **An inspector** examines the home.
- **A title or escrow company**, or in some states an attorney, handles the closing paperwork.

Who does what differs by state. Ask your agent how it works where you live.

## What are the common mistakes?

- Shopping before getting pre-approved.
- Stretching to the top of the budget and leaving no cushion.
- Skipping the inspection to look more attractive.
- Changing jobs, opening credit cards or financing a car before closing.
- Forgetting costs beyond the mortgage, like upkeep and insurance.

## Where do I start?

Start with what you can comfortably pay each month, not the highest price a lender might approve. Then look at homes. When one stands out, try See My Payment for this home.

Nothing here is a promise of approval or a price. It is a map. Your agent, a lender and an attorney where needed can fill in the details for your area.
