# The hidden costs of owning a detached home

*Owning a home*

By your listing agent

*Educational information, not financial or legal advice*

A detached home gives you space and control. It also makes you responsible for everything, from the roof to the sprinklers. None of this is meant to scare you. It is meant to help you plan.

## What are the big-ticket items?

These last a long time, but they do wear out, and replacing them is expensive:

- **Roof.** Age and material matter. Ask how old it is.
- **Heating and cooling.** A furnace or air conditioner has a limited life.
- **Water heater.** Often replaced about every ten years or so, more or less.
- **Windows, siding and paint.** Exterior care protects everything else.
- **Plumbing and electrical.** Older systems may need updates.

Ask for the age of each at the open house. If they are old, plan for replacing them.

## What about the yard and outside?

You'll be paying in time or money for lawn care, trees, fencing, driveways, decks and drainage. Fences and retaining walls need repairs too.

## What depends on where I live?

Costs vary by region. Examples include:

- Snow removal and winter heating in cold climates
- Irrigation systems, water use and drought rules in dry ones
- Pest control, such as termites or rodents, in many areas
- Storm, flood or wildfire exposure, which affects insurance
- Sump pumps, gutters and foundation care in wet climates

Your agent can tell you what is typical in your area.

## How much should I set aside?

As a rule of thumb, plan to keep about 1% of a home's value a year for maintenance. On a home worth a few hundred thousand, that is a few thousand dollars a year. Some years you spend nothing, and then one repair takes it all. An older home may need more. It is a guideline, not a guarantee.

A simple habit is to move money into a separate savings account every month.

## Is a home warranty necessary?

No, it's optional. A home warranty is a service contract that may cover some repairs to systems and appliances, for a fee. Coverage limits, exclusions and service fees vary a lot, so read the terms. Sometimes a seller offers one as part of the sale. An inspection is a better way to learn the home's condition than a warranty.

## What is not in the monthly payment?

See My Payment for this home includes the mortgage-related costs. Upkeep, utilities and the repairs above are on top, so keep them in mind when deciding what you're comfortable paying. Also compare insurance costs, which can differ by home and area.

Your agent, an inspector or a contractor can help you estimate costs for a specific home.
